Estimates from each run's second-by-second data on Strava. Critical speed is the fastest pace you could hold for a long time, and D′ the distance you can run "above" it before you're spent; both come from your best efforts of 2–20 minutes in the last 6 months (the critical speed model). Speed work adds up (speed − critical speed) × seconds over everything faster than critical speed: faster running counts more, by exactly how much faster, whatever your heart rate did. "Tanks" is that divided by your D′. Top-speed running is metres at 90% of your top speed or faster: the leg-stress side. The line each week is 1.5 × your average over the 4 weeks before (the acute-to-chronic workload rule from injury research; debated, so a guide), never lower than 2 tanks of D′ for speed work or 500 m of top-speed running. It rises as you build up gradually; a sudden jump goes over it. Stride speed is how fast your strides are at their best 5 seconds. Aerobic efficiency is easy-run speed per heartbeat.
